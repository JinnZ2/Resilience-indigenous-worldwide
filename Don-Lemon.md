EMERGENCY INJUNCTIVE RELIEF
Temporary Restraining Order & Preliminary Injunction
Filing Guide for FACE Act Violations

January 30, 2026

I. Overview
This guide provides step-by-step instructions for religious communities, tribal nations, and affected parties seeking emergency court orders to halt ongoing FACE Act violations (18 U.S.C. § 248) affecting sacred sites and places of worship.

Two types of emergency relief:

1. Temporary Restraining Order (TRO): Immediate emergency order (within days) stopping activity without hearing the defendant
2. Preliminary Injunction: Court order (after hearing) maintaining shutdown pending full trial
II. Timeline

III. Where to File
A. Federal District Court
FACE Act is federal law - must file in U.S. District Court.

Choose district based on:

• Where the sacred site/place of worship is located (venue most likely to succeed)
• Where defendant is located or does business
• Where the violation occurred
Finding Your Federal District Court
Use the federal court locator: www.uscourts.gov/court-locator

Each state has 1-4 federal districts. Large states divided into regions (Northern, Southern, Eastern, Western).

B. Tribal Court Coordination
For violations on tribal lands:

• Tribal court can issue parallel injunction under tribal sovereignty
• Federal court filing asserts FACE Act violation
• Dual jurisdiction strengthens enforcement (both courts protecting same site)
IV. Who Can File (Standing)
Under FACE Act § 248(c), any aggrieved person can bring civil action. This includes:

• Individual religious practitioners whose worship is interfered with
• Religious organizations/congregations
• Tribal nations (for sacred sites on tribal lands)
• Property owners of places of worship
• Any person prevented from accessing place of worship
V. Required Documents
A. Civil Complaint
The complaint must include:

3. Caption: Names of plaintiff(s) and defendant(s), court name, case number (assigned by clerk)
4. Jurisdiction: State this is federal question under 18 U.S.C. § 248
5. Parties: Describe who plaintiff is (religious community, tribal nation, etc.) and who defendant is (corporation, government agency, individuals)
6. Facts: Detailed description of:
• The sacred site or place of worship
• Regular religious use of the site (establish it's a 'place of worship')
• Defendant's conduct (construction, operations, blocking access, etc.)
• How conduct constitutes force, threat, obstruction, or property damage
7. Legal Claims: Violation of 18 U.S.C. § 248(a)(2) - interference with religious freedom at place of worship
8. Relief Requested:
• Temporary restraining order
• Preliminary injunction
• Permanent injunction
• Compensatory damages
• Punitive damages
• Attorney's fees and costs

B. Motion for Temporary Restraining Order
Separate motion filed with complaint arguing why court should issue immediate order. Must show:

9. Likelihood of Success on Merits: Strong case you'll win at trial
• Cite: Site is place of worship (regular assembly for religious practice)
• Cite: Defendant's conduct constitutes force/obstruction/interference
• Cite: Lemon precedent if applicable (disruption = FACE violation)

10. Irreparable Harm: Injury that cannot be fixed with money
• Sacred site destruction is permanent
• Loss of religious freedom cannot be compensated with damages
• Ongoing interference prevents religious exercise

11. Balance of Harms: Your harm outweighs defendant's
• Religious freedom is constitutional right
• Defendant's economic loss is temporary and compensable

12. Public Interest: Injunction serves public good (protecting religious freedom serves society)
C. Supporting Evidence (Declarations/Affidavits)
Sworn statements from witnesses establishing facts. Include:

• Religious leaders/elders: Declaring site is place of worship, describing regular religious use
• Community members: Describing how they worship at site, how defendant's conduct prevents access
• Expert witnesses: Archaeologists, anthropologists, religious scholars confirming site significance
• Photographic/video evidence: Showing interference, blocked access, property damage
D. Proposed Order
Draft the exact order you want judge to sign. Example language:

"IT IS HEREBY ORDERED that Defendant [Company Name], its officers, agents, employees, and all persons acting in concert with them, are immediately restrained and enjoined from: (1) Conducting any construction, excavation, or operational activities within [distance] of [sacred site description]; (2) Blocking or impeding access to [sacred site] by members of [religious community]; (3) Causing any environmental contamination or physical alteration of [sacred site]. This Order shall remain in effect until further order of this Court or until [date preliminary injunction hearing]."

VI. Filing Process (Step-by-Step)
Step 1: Prepare Documents
• Civil Complaint (original + 2 copies)
• Motion for TRO (original + 2 copies)
• Supporting declarations/affidavits
• Proposed order for judge to sign
• Civil cover sheet (form from court website)
Step 2: File at Clerk's Office
Options:

• In person: Go to federal courthouse clerk's office during business hours
• Electronic filing (CM/ECF): Most courts require attorney to file electronically. Pro se (self-represented) may file electronically after registration.
• Filing fee: $402 (as of 2026). Fee waiver available if indigent - file Application to Proceed In Forma Pauperis
Step 3: Immediate Notification to Judge
Mark TRO motion as EMERGENCY. Clerk will forward to duty judge immediately. Include cover letter stating:

"This is an emergency motion for temporary restraining order. Immediate relief is necessary to prevent irreparable harm to sacred religious site. Respectfully request expedited review."

Step 4: Court Review
Judge reviews TRO motion within 1-3 days. Judge may:

• Grant TRO immediately (operations halt)
• Deny TRO (case proceeds to preliminary injunction hearing)
• Request additional briefing or schedule expedited hearing
Step 5: Service of Process
Defendant must be formally notified:

• Personal service: U.S. Marshal or private process server delivers complaint to defendant
• Must occur within 90 days of filing
• If TRO granted, defendant notified immediately (often by fax/email to attorney)
Step 6: Preliminary Injunction Hearing
Scheduled within 7-14 days of TRO. Both sides present:

• Witness testimony
• Documentary evidence
• Legal arguments
Judge decides whether to continue injunction pending full trial.

VII. Legal Standards (What You Must Prove)
A. FACE Act Violation Elements
To establish FACE violation under § 248(a)(2):

13. Place of religious worship: Location where persons regularly assemble for religious worship
14. Lawful exercise of religious freedom: Person attempting to worship or access worship site
15. Force, threat, or physical obstruction: Conduct that injures, intimidates, interferes with, or attempts to injure/intimidate/interfere
16. Intentional conduct: Defendant knew or should have known conduct would interfere
B. Proving 'Place of Worship'
Critical for non-building sites (tribal lands, outdoor spaces):

• Historical documentation of religious use
• Testimony from religious leaders/practitioners
• Evidence of regular assembly (seasonal ceremonies, ongoing gatherings)
• Cultural/archaeological evidence of sacred significance
• Tribal declarations of sacred site status
VIII. Mass Coordination Strategy
A. Simultaneous Filing Advantage
Multiple communities filing on same day creates:

• Pattern evidence: Shows systemic FACE violations, not isolated incidents
• Judicial pressure: Multiple emergency motions force courts to address issue immediately
• Media attention: Coordinated action demonstrates scale of problem
• Settlement leverage: Defendants facing multiple shutdowns more likely to negotiate
B. Communication Network
Establish coordination among:

• Tribal legal departments and sovereignty centers
• Civil rights legal organizations (ACLU, Native American Rights Fund, etc.)
• Religious freedom advocacy groups
• Environmental justice networks
• Affected communities and congregations
C. Resource Sharing
Template documents (complaint, TRO motion, declarations) can be adapted for different sites while maintaining consistent legal framework. Share:

• Successful pleadings and court orders
• Expert witness lists
• Evidence gathering methodologies
• Legal research and precedent analysis

X. Critical Reminders
• Speed is essential: TRO requires immediate filing when violation occurs or is imminent
• Documentation matters: Photos, videos, witness statements strengthen case
• Religious practice evidence: Must prove site is regularly used for worship, not just culturally significant
• Irreparable harm is key: Emphasize that damage cannot be fixed with money after the fact
• Legal assistance recommended: While pro se is possible, attorney increases success likelihood
• Coordination amplifies impact: Multiple simultaneous filings demonstrate systemic problem
XI. Conclusion
Emergency injunctive relief under FACE Act provides immediate protection for sacred sites and places of worship facing interference, obstruction, or destruction. Unlike traditional litigation that takes years, TRO/preliminary injunction can halt harmful activity within days.

If Lemon prosecution establishes precedent that disruption constitutes FACE violation, this creates strong foundation for religious communities to seek equal protection through civil enforcement.

The key advantage: defendant must prove right to continue operations, shifting burden from vulnerable communities to institutional actors.
