# Indigenous Impact — 1854 Ceded Territory

## Who is affected

The proposed Twin Metals sulfide mine sits inside the 1854 Treaty
ceded territory, where three federally recognized Ojibwe Bands retain
reserved usufructuary rights:

| Band | Enrollment | Reservation distance from mine |
|------|-----------:|-------------------------------|
| Bois Forte Band of Chippewa | ~3,400 | 40 mi NW (Nett Lake / Vermilion) |
| Grand Portage Band of Lake Superior Chippewa | ~1,100 | 100 mi NE (Lake Superior shore) |
| Fond du Lac Band of Lake Superior Chippewa | ~4,200 | 80 mi S (Duluth / St. Louis R.) |
| **Total enrollment** | **~8,700** | |

These Bands — and the 1854 Treaty Authority that administers their
off-reservation rights — hunt, fish, gather, and harvest manoomin
throughout the Rainy River and upper Lake Superior watersheds, which
is the exact surface-water footprint the mine would contaminate.

## What is at stake

### Manoomin (wild rice) — a protected relative, not a resource

Manoomin (*Zizania palustris*) is the sacred food of the Anishinaabe
migration story (the Seventh Fire prophecy). It is also, under
Minnesota law and tribal law, a legal subject:

- **Treaty property** — reserved under the 1854 Treaty as an
  off-reservation gathering right.
- **Rights-bearing entity** — the White Earth Band's 2018 "Rights of
  Manoomin" law and the 1855 Treaty Authority's 2018 resolution
  recognize manoomin as a legal person with the right to exist,
  flourish, and regenerate.
- **Sulfate-intolerant** — MN Rule 7050.0224 sets a 10 mg/L sulfate
  standard specifically to protect manoomin. Above ~10 mg/L, seedling
  germination collapses; above ~50 mg/L the rice stand dies.

The model tracks 18,400 acres of manoomin at risk within the direct
contamination plume.

### Water, wells, and the land

- **12,400 residents** live within 20 km of the mine; **84%** depend
  on private wells. Wells are the first casualty once the water
  table (2.1 m) intersects an acid plume.
- **234,000 acres** of boreal forest corridor, including peatlands
  that methylate mercury at 31% efficiency — a biogeochemical trap
  that turns inorganic Hg from the ore into neurotoxic methylmercury
  that bioaccumulates 2.7 × 10⁶ × in lake trout.
- **1,175 interconnected lakes** of the BWCA. 78% are hydrologically
  linked. Residence time averages 3.2 years, so contamination
  propagates lake-to-lake over decades and never flushes.

impossible to exercise.

## Why this is a taking, not a trade-off

The mine's direct employment figure is **700 jobs for 20 years**. The
model's net jobs column shows a peak loss of **−13,440 to −17,616**
once tourism and timber collapse. That is before counting:

- The loss of a 1,100-year-old Anishinaabe food-sovereignty practice.
- Involuntary relocation of up to **8,060** residents (tailings-failure
  peak) from wells that can no longer be used.
- A sulfate plume that, once started, runs on negative Gibbs free
  energy and microbial catalysis for **≥ 300 years** regardless of
  whether the mine closes.

Under UNDRIP Article 29 and the Free, Prior, and Informed Consent
(FPIC) standard, a federal permitting action that produces these
outcomes without affirmative tribal consent is not a regulatory
decision — it is a confiscation of treaty property. The same SEC Item
103 and EU CSDDD disclosure logic this repo uses for Venezuela applies
directly here: every institutional investor in Antofagasta and every
hedge fund buying Twin Metals debt carries undisclosed liability
exposure equal to the Trail Smelter NPV in the model.
